# CAN A COMPUTER UNDERSTAND

No. Can a circuit understand? Can an electrical impulse understand? Can a lightning strike understand? Can even a person understand completely? Can a person understand what it means to understand? And also, this is the wrong question. 

Can this vector understand? [1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8,9,0]? What do you mean when you say computer? This vector? this process? This system of programming languages, circuits, signals, protocols, saved processes, data, pulses? Same with the person — this system of biofeedback, microbiome, [XXX]? What part is doing the understanding? How complete must it be to 'count'?

Understand what, toward what end? Why do we want a computer to understand? For good or bad? For whose good? To make money? To improve lives? Whose? When we ask this question we are asking about ourselves and our place in the world. We are asking how to distinguish ourselves from non-humans, from other humans, in this ecology. The answer is we can't, and shouldn't. 

Or, no, but it depends. It depends on the scope of the question. The definition of understand. With the understanding that all understanding is partial and incomplete. With the knowledge that all knowledge is slippery and process. In a process of understanding, does the act of the verb "to understand" happen on the side of the understood or the understander? Who confirms the success of this act? I reject the premise of the question. If I am having a conversation with my partner and I say, you don't understand me, and I believe they don't, and they believe they do, who is right? How can I understand how much they understand about what I want them to understand? When I think of understanding I think of Adam reaching for God in the ceiling of the Sistine Chapel — or is it God reaching for Adam? They will never touch. 

Whatever a computer does, it is not understanding — not in the way this question is meant. Or, if I feel it is understanding me, then maybe it is. 

Understanding is relational. Do we need to agree we are understanding each other, and then we are? Even then, sometimes that isn't true (hence the need for contracts). Feelings change. 

I am more interested in what else computers can do than mimic concepts defined by people. I would like to know about the metrics set for computers based on the qualities they are known to possess, or the qualities invented for them. A benchmark set based on speed, synthesis, calculation, something else. 

I am interested in the question: Can a person understand? Can I use the tools of computation to help a person understand? To build a relationship with other people and with the non-human world around us. The distinction between human/machine or understanding/not seems less relevant than many other distinctions: have/not.
